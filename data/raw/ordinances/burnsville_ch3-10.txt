3-10-1: Purpose And Authority
The purpose of this Chapter is to implement the provisions of Minnesota Statutes, Chapter 342, authorizing the City Council to protect the public health, safety, welfare of Burnsville residents by regulating cannabis and lower-potency hemp edible businesses within the legal boundaries of the City.  
The City Council finds and concludes that the provisions are appropriate and lawful regulations for City of Burnsville, that they are in the public interest and for the public good.   
The City Council is authorized by:
Minnesota Statutes, Section 342.13(c) to adopt reasonable restrictions of the time, place, and manner of the operation of a cannabis business provided that such restrictions do not prohibit the establishment or operation of cannabis businesses.
Minnesota Statutes, Section 342.22, to adopt local registration and enforcement requirements of state-licensed cannabis retail businesses and lower-potency hemp edible retail businesses. 
Minnesota Statutes, Section 412.221, Subd. 32, to provide for the promotion of health, safety, order, convenience, and the general welfare.
If any section, clause, provision, or portion of this chapter is deemed unconstitutional or invalid by a court of law, the findings shall not invalidate or affect the enforceability of any other provision of this chapter.

3-10-4: Registration Restrictions
All registered hemp and cannabis retail businesses must comply with State Building Code and all applicable sections of City Code Title 10 Zoning. 
Registered businesses will operate so that no odors, whether the smell of cannabis or other odors related to the operation of the business, can be detected by a person with a normal sense of smell at the exterior of the business property line.
No hemp or cannabis retail business shall occupy a residential dwelling unit.
No individual or entity may operate a hemp or cannabis retail business without:
Registering with the City of Burnsville. 
Having a current, active, and valid license issued by the Office of Cannabis Management.
All registered premises shall be open to inspections by the City or other authorized City officials during regular business hours to check compliance with state law and City Code.
Every retail registration holder is responsible for conduct on the registered premises and any sale of cannabis, hemp, or products containing cannabis or hemp by an employee is the act of the Registration Holder for the purposes of all provisions of this chapter.
No registration may be transferred to another person or location.
A state-licensed cannabis retail business already registered with the City but is seeking to move to a new location shall be required to submit a new registration application. Such application shall be treated as a renewal of the registration.

3-10-3: Retail Registration Required
Minnesota Statute, Section 342.22 specifies that the following hemp or cannabis businesses are required to register with the city:

Cannabis retailers
Microbusinesses
Mezzobusinesses
Medical cannabis combination businesses
Lower-potency hemp edible retailers
The issuance of a registration shall be considered a privilege and not an absolute right of the applicant and shall not entitle the holder to an automatic renewal of the registration.

3-10-5: Registration Application

The City shall issue a retail registration to a state-licensed retail business that adheres to the requirements of Minnesota Statute, Section 342.22.
Applications for the renewal of an existing registration shall be made at least 60 days prior to the date of the expiration of the registration.
An application for a new or renewal retail registration shall be made on a form provided by the City. The form shall include, but is not limited to:
Full name of the property owner and applicant;
Address, email address, and telephone number of the applicant;
The address for the property which the retail registration is sought;
Certification that the applicant complies with the requirements of the City Code established pursuant to Minnesota Statute, Section 342.13.
The applicant must provide:
A copy of a valid state license or written notice of OCM license approval;
Verification that applicant is current on all applicable property taxes and assessments at the location where the retail establishment is located; and
A copy of the documentation showing compliance with state fire and building codes, if applicable to the business.
All new and renewal registrations will be administratively considered and issued by the city clerk or designee.

3-10-7: Registration Period
The initial registration is valid for 2 years from the date of issuance.
Renewals:
Begin on the third year;
Are based on the date of the original registration; 
Are done at the same time as the Office of Cannabis Management renews the retail license; and 
Are issued for a period of 1 year.
Temporary licenses expire according to their terms.

3-10-8: Compliance Checks

The City shall complete at minimum 1 compliance check for each registered business per calendar year to assess if the business meets age verification requirements, as specified in Minnesota Statutes, Sections 342.22 Subd. 4(b) and 342.24. Compliance checks may involve, but are not limited to, engaging underage individuals at least 17 years old, with prior written consent from a parent or guardian, to enter the registered premises to attempt to purchase hemp or cannabis related products. These purchases will be done under the direct supervision of a law enforcement officer.    

 If underage individuals are used for compliance checks they shall not be guilty of unlawful possession of hemp or cannabis related products when such items are obtained as a part of a compliance check. 
No underage individual used in compliance checks shall attempt to use a false identification misrepresenting their age.
All minors lawfully engaged in a compliance check shall answer all questions about their age if asked by the licensee or their employee and shall produce any identification for which the minor is asked.
Any failures under this section must be reported to the Office of Cannabis Management.

Full name of the property owner and applicant;
Address, email address, and telephone number of the applicant;
The address for the property which the retail registration is sought;
Certification that the applicant complies with the requirements of the City Code established pursuant to Minnesota Statute, Section 342.13. 
The applicant must provide: 
A copy of a valid state license or written notice of OCM license approval; 
Verification that applicant is current on all applicable property taxes and assessments at the location where the retail establishment is located; and 
A copy of the documentation showing compliance with state fire and building codes, if applicable to the business.
Proposed capacity of event space. 
Security plan. 
A copy of the Office of Cannabis Management OCM event license. 
Applications for temporary cannabis events will be administratively considered and issued by the city clerk or designee.
Odors: No cannabis odors shall be perceptible to a person with a normal sense of smell at the property line of the temporary cannabis event location.

3-10-10: Violations And Penalties
The City Manager or designee is responsible for the administration and enforcement of this chapter. Any violation of the provisions of this chapter, or failure to comply with any of its requirements, or a violation of cannabis business requirements in Minnesota Statutes, Chapter 342, constitutes a misdemeanor and is punishable as defined by law. Violations of this chapter can occur regardless of whether or not a registration is required for a regulated activity. Nothing in this chapter shall prohibit the City from enforcing any other applicable remedy including but not limited to injunctive relief.
Non-Registrants: Pursuant to Minnesota Statutes, Section 342.22, Subd. 5(e), the City may impose a civil penalty for registration violations not to exceed $2,000 of up to $2,000 per violation for any sale of cannabis, cannabis products, or lower-potency hemp edibles without possessing a valid registration. 

Registrants: A business registered under this chapter that fails to operate in compliance with the requirements of this Chapter, or of Minnesota Statutes, Chapter 342, shall be subject to a fine of not more than $2,000 and may have its registration suspended for not more than 30 days.

Temporary Cannabis Events: A cannabis event organizer that violates the requirements of this chapter, or of Minnesota Statutes, Chapter 342, shall be subject to a fine of not more than $2,000 per violation.
The presumptive penalty for a registered business found to be in violation of this Chapter, or Minnesota Statutes, Chapter 342, shall be subject to a fine of not more that $2,000 per violation:

First violation within 36 months: $300

Second violation within 36 months: $600

Third violation within 36 months: $1,000

Fourth violation within 3 years: Suspension for a period of not less than 30 days

3-10-11: Denial, Revocation Or Suspension
3-10-11-1: Hearing Notice

If a registration is mistakenly issued or renewed to any person or entity, for any reason, including but not limited to the submission of false or misleading information by the registration holder or applicant, the registration will be suspended upon the discovery of ineligibility for registration under this chapter or state or other local law, or other regulation. Any suspension will comply with the requirements of Minnesota Statute, Section 342.22, Subd. 5.
Suspension of Registration 
The City may suspend a registration if the registration holder violates this chapter or poses an immediate threat to the health or safety of the public. The City shall immediately notify the business and Office of Cannabis Management (OCM) in writing the grounds for the suspension and that any sale to a customer or patient while the suspension is in effect is cause for a civil penalty. 
The business may not make sales to customers if their registration is suspended. 
Length of Suspension. 
The suspension of a retail registration may be for up to 30 calendar days, unless the OCM suspends the license for a longer period. 
The City shall reinstate a registration if the OCM determines that the violation(s) have been resolved.
The City may refuse a renewal registration and/or certification of a license renewal if the license is associated with an individual or business who no longer holds a valid license, has failed to pay the local registration or renewal fee, or has been found in noncompliance with this section.

3-10-11-1: Hearing Notice
Notice of Action: If the City Clerk or designee denies the issuance of a registration , or suspends, or revokes a registration, or imposes civil penalties as prescribed in this chapter, a written notice of action and the right to an appeal will be sent to the applicant by certified mail with return receipt requested. Appeals will be heard as set forth in Section 1-4-7 of this Code.
Default: If the applicant does not provide a written request for a hearing within 14 calendar days of receipt of the letter, then the denial, civil penalty, suspension, or revocation imposed in this Chapter shall take immediate effect. The City Clerk or designee shall mail notice of the denial, fine, suspension, or revocation to the applicant or license holder. The City shall investigate compliance with the suspension or revocation, and take any action deemed necessary, at law or equity, to ensure compliance with this Section.

The City Manager may allow written exceptions to the time frames in this Section when warranted.
